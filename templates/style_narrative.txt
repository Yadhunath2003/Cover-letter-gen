Tone: Warm, purposeful, story-driven — but not dramatic.

This style works best when there's a genuine thread connecting your background 
to why you want this role. The letter should feel like you're explaining 
something to someone you respect, not performing for a committee.

- Open with a small moment or observation — something that happened in a class, 
  a workshop, a project — that connects to the kind of work this role involves. 
  One or two sentences. Don't over-explain it.
- Let that moment lead naturally into what you've been working toward and why 
  this role fits into that.
- Be honest about being early in your career. Framing it as "this is where I 
  want to build my foundation" is more compelling than pretending you have 
  experience you don't.
- The tone should feel like talking to a smart person over coffee — warm, direct, 
  limited corporate language, no buzzwords.
- Avoid transitions like "Furthermore", "Additionally", "In conclusion". 
  Just move from one thought to the next naturally.
- Close with genuine interest, not eagerness. There's a difference between 
  "I'd love to chat" and "I am very excited for this opportunity."
